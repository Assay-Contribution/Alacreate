# Early Adopter Brainstorm

*As of 2026-09-19*

## Overview

The best early adopters are places where hourly tracking is already resented or already breaking down — not places starting from zero:

- **Remote-first startups (seed–Series B, <100 people)** — no timezone overlap for standups, culture already async, leadership already skeptical of hours-as-productivity
- **Creative/consulting agencies moving off billable hours** — value-based or retainer pricing removes the legal/billing reason to track hours at all
- **Engineering-heavy startups with handbook-driven, async-first cultures** (GitLab/Basecamp-style) — already primed to trust written self-report over surveillance
- **DAOs and Web3 contributor collectives** — literally compensate by contribution already, no incumbent tool fits their comp model
- **Nonprofits and mission-driven orgs** — "contribution to the mission" is a more natural framing than billable hours, and budgets favor cheap tools
- **Flat/manager-light orgs** — fewer people to chase timesheets, so a lightweight weekly ritual is an easier sell than building out a management layer
- **Fractional COOs / ops consultants implementing OKRs at startups** — they're often the ones introducing new rituals and could become a channel, not just a user
- **Mid-market teams outgrowing 15Five/Lattice-lite usage** — already sold on weekly check-ins, just want something less HR-heavy and cheaper
- **Staffing agencies and contractor networks piloting an alternative to timesheets** for clients who care about outcomes, not hours logged
